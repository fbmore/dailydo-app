Privacy Policy

Last updated: 11 November 2026

DailyDo is built so that your tasks, notes, and documents stay yours. The app has no accounts and no analytics. Your data lives on your devices and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud account. The developer never sees it. The one exception is a page you choose to publish to the web, which is public by design.

In short  No sign-up. No tracking. No ads. No analytics. Nothing you capture reaches anyone else unless you share it, collaborate on it, or publish it as a web page. If you buy DailyDo Pro, an anonymous purchase record is kept by our subscription provider, RevenueCat.

What DailyDo stores

Everything you create in DailyDo is stored locally on your device: tasks (with due dates, reminders, repeat rules and completion history), notes, links, timers, checklists, podcasts you follow and your listening position, tags, and attached documents, photos, scans, videos and voice recordings. DailyDo may also cache phone numbers, email addresses and names it detects in your own text so it can offer actions on them.

If you are signed in to iCloud, DailyDo syncs this data to your own private iCloud account (Apple's CloudKit private database for the container iCloud.com.fbmore.DD, plus that container's iCloud Drive folder for attached files). Your settings sync through iCloud key-value storage. This keeps your iPhone, iPad and Mac up to date and restores your data if you reinstall the app. The developer has no access to your private iCloud data. Two features you choose to use place data elsewhere: an item you collaborate on is shared through iCloud with only the people you invite, and a page you publish goes to a public database (see Published pages).

Widgets and the share extension read a small summary (item titles, due times, pinned and timer state) from a container shared only with DailyDo on the same device.

On-device intelligence

DailyDo's smart features run on your device using Apple's built-in frameworks. No AI service outside your device is used.

  • Smart capture: working out whether text is a task, note, link or timer, and pulling out dates and times.
  • Note titling: summarizing long text into a short title with Apple's on-device language model.
  • Document scanning and text recognition: reading text in scans and photos.
  • Voice transcription: converting dictation, recordings and podcast episodes to text with Apple's Speech framework. When on-device recognition isn't available for your language, Apple may process the audio on its servers under Apple's privacy terms.

Network requests the app makes

DailyDo talks to the internet only to do things you asked for. Apart from publishing a page, none of these requests go to the developer.

  • iCloud sync: your data travels between your devices through Apple's iCloud, encrypted in transit and tied to your Apple Account.
  • Links you save: DailyDo loads the page directly from that website to read its title and preview, just as a browser would.
  • Podcasts and feeds you follow: episodes and updates download directly from the publisher's servers. DailyDo also looks up a podcast's title in Apple's public iTunes search to find its artwork. YouTube channel feeds and video embeds load from YouTube (embeds use the privacy-enhanced youtube-nocookie.com domain).
  • Book lookups: when an item contains an ISBN, DailyDo can look up the book's title on isbnsearch.org, sending only that ISBN.
  • Published pages: when you publish, the page is uploaded to DailyDo's public iCloud database, and the app asks our page server for each page's view count. See below.
  • Purchases: see below.

Published pages

You can select items and publish them as a web page at an unlisted getdailydo.com/v/… address. Nothing is published unless you tap Publish, and the app shows you what will be public first.

  • What's published: the page title, the optional message and name you enter, and the selected items' titles, text, checklists, due dates, tags, links and attached photos, videos, recordings (with their transcripts) and files. Photos are resized and videos re-encoded first. Reminders, alarms and other settings are not published.
  • Where it's stored: in the public database of DailyDo's iCloud container (iCloud.com.fbmore.DD), created with your iCloud account. Apple identifies the creator only by an anonymous ID; we don't see your Apple Account name or email. Only you can change a page.
  • Who can see it: anyone who has the link, without an account. Pages aren't listed or indexed, but anyone you give the link to can pass it on and download the files. Because pages are public, the developer can see them too, and may look at one that's reported.
  • Our page server: viewers load pages through a small server we run on Cloudflare. It reads the page from iCloud, builds the link preview, and keeps one number per page: how many times it was opened. It stores no IP addresses, cookies or details about viewers. Cloudflare processes requests under its privacy policy. The page itself contains no analytics.
  • How long it lasts: until the link expires (7 days, or up to 90 with DailyDo Pro), you stop sharing it, or you delete its items in the app. Expired pages and their files are deleted every night. We may also remove a page that breaks the Terms of Use.

Purchases and DailyDo Pro

DailyDo is free for up to 50 active items. DailyDo Pro is sold as an in-app subscription or one-time purchase through Apple. Apple processes all payments; we never see your name, email, Apple Account or payment details.

To know whether Pro is unlocked on all your devices, DailyDo uses RevenueCat, a subscription-management service. RevenueCat receives your App Store purchase receipt and a random, anonymous app user ID generated by the app, along with basic technical details such as app version, device model, OS version and country/currency for pricing. This data is not linked to your identity, is not used for advertising or tracking, and is used only to provide and manage your purchase. The developer can see these anonymous purchase records in RevenueCat's dashboard.

This website

getdailydo.com uses Foliàire Analytics to count page views, scroll depth and time on page. It sets no cookies, collects no personal data, honours Do Not Track and does not follow you across other sites. This applies only to the website; the DailyDo app contains no analytics.

What we do not collect

  • No account, name, email or phone number is required or collected.
  • No analytics, usage tracking, crash reporting or advertising SDKs of any kind in the app.
  • No advertising identifier and no tracking across apps or websites.
  • No location data.
  • None of your tasks, notes, links, files or recordings are sent to the developer, except what you choose to publish as a web page.

Permissions, only when needed

DailyDo asks for each permission the first time a feature needs it, and works without any of them; only the related feature is unavailable.

  • Notifications and Alarms: reminders for tasks you schedule, and alarms that can break through Focus if you choose them.
  • Camera: scanning documents, taking photos and videos for an item, reading QR codes.
  • Microphone and Speech Recognition: dictation, voice recordings, video sound, and transcripts.
  • Photos: attaching photos and videos you pick, and saving an image when you tap Save to Photos.
  • Contacts: saving a phone number or email found in an item as a new contact, when you ask.
  • Files (Mac: Desktop, Documents, Downloads): importing files you choose.

Sharing

DailyDo shares only when you ask it to, in one of three ways:

  • Share as a link or text: DailyDo packs the content into the link or message itself and hands it to the system share sheet. Nothing is uploaded, and anything you send can't be taken back.
  • Collaborate: the item is shared through iCloud with the people you invite, who can see it (and edit it, if you allow). Stop sharing at any time.
  • Publish as a web page: a public page anyone with the link can open, as described in Published pages.

Deleting your data

Delete individual items in the app at any time; deletions sync to your other devices, and deleting an item also deletes any page it was published on. Stop sharing a page at any time from Settings → Published Pages. To remove everything, delete DailyDo from all your devices and then remove its iCloud data in Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → DailyDo → Delete Data from iCloud (on Mac: System Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage). To ask us to delete the anonymous purchase record RevenueCat keeps, email us with the date and type of your purchase.

Children's privacy

DailyDo is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any personal information from them.
